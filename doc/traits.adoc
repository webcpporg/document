[#document-traits]
== Traits

A document's traits choose what it stores and how: the {cpp} types of its scalars, the encoding of
its strings, how it writes bytes, and the order of an object's keys. `wd::basic_document<Traits,
Storage>` takes them first; `wd::document` is `wd::basic_document<wd::default_traits, wd::owned>`,
and its handles `wd::value` and `wd::const_value` are those of the default traits too.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Member | `wd::default_traits` | `wd::js_traits`

| `boolean`
| `bool`
| `bool`

| `integer`
| `std::int64_t`
| `std::int64_t`

| `number`
| `boost::decimal::decimal128_t`: exact, 34 significant digits
| `double`: JavaScript's own numbers

| `string`
| `wd::utf8`
| `wd::utf16`, as JavaScript holds strings

| `date`
| `wd::date_ms`
| `wd::date_ms`

| `bytes`
| `wd::base64`
| `wd::javascript_bytes`, as `JSON.stringify` writes a Uint8Array

| `object`
| `wd::javascript_order`
| `wd::javascript_order`

| `array`
| `wd::dense`
| `wd::dense`
|===

`js_traits` derive from the default traits, and a program's traits do the same, changing what they
need:

[source]
----
include::{examples}/document_traits.cpp[tag=define,indent=0]
----

<<document-order>> parses a text with both, and <<document-strings-bytes>> writes bytes with the
second. The concept `wd::document_traits` says what traits must give, and a document of traits
that do not satisfy it does not compile. This version fixes `boolean`, `integer` and `date` to
`bool`, `std::int64_t` and `wd::date_ms`; `number` is the traits' choice, a type that satisfies
`wd::stored_scalar`, trivially copyable, of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bytes, with a `wd::scalar_codec` that
stores it the same way on every target and a `wd::number_codec` that reads it from JSON text and
writes it as JavaScript writes numbers. `string`, `bytes` and `object` are one of the encodings and
policies <<document-strings>> and <<document-order>> list, and `array` is `wd::dense`, the one
array policy.

[#document-traits-identity]
=== Traits are part of the image

A document's image records its traits: its header holds a tag that hashes the size and the codec
of each scalar, the encoding, the bytes policy and the object policy, with the sizes of the tree's
nodes. An image written with other traits is refused with `errc::incompatible_document`, never read
wrongly (<<document-images-refused>>). Copies between documents need the same traits, and a
document's handles carry them in their type, so a handle of one traits cannot be given to a
document of another.
